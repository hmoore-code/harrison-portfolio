The anatomy of our platform’s engagement

Between 2017 and 2022, we ran Worker Feedback Club on 13 construction sites, including major projects like HS2, gathering feedback from hard-to-reach workers in three different ways.

  1. 1

    Visit site

    Contractors invited us because of low productivity and costly rework. They wanted to understand the problem through workers’ eyes.

  3. 2

    Collect feedback 3 ways

    • Face-to-face
    • Video interviews on iPads
    • Our anonymous web app

    Questions were translated into every language needed, so all workers could contribute.

  5. 3

    Analyse feedback

    Site rankings, on aspects ranging from safety to cleanliness to respectfulness, were created using scores and comments.

  7. 4

    Produce impact report

    Written reports backed by data were produced for each site.

  9. 5

    Make recommendations

    Long-term goals and “quick wins” were put forward to make sites safer, more organised, and more cooperative for all.

What happened next

Many of our recommendations (eg, icons on signs for workers with little English, a translated glossary of common site words, and easier-to-read minutes from worker meetings) were adopted by Willmott Dixon, whilst Multiplex audited all of its dry-lining subcontractors to improve productivity and safety.

Our anonymous app found problems that had been hidden

We asked workers to agree or disagree with the statement, “I feel safe being on this site.” The feedback was markedly different depending on whether it was public or private.

Rated from 1 (disagree strongly) to 5 (agree strongly). Tier 1 contractor, London site, 2021.

What happened next

Insights like these led us to build features that let workers report problems anonymously, in any language, directly to site managers, and continue the conversation until problems were resolved.
